## How P2P Payment Platforms Actually Work (And Why Most Are Outdated)

Understanding how P2P payment platforms operate reveals why traditional systems create unnecessary friction and costs for users.

### Traditional P2P Architecture: The Five-Step Bottleneck

Most established P2P payment apps follow a centralized model that involves multiple intermediaries:

1. **Authentication**: User credentials are verified through the app
2. **Authorization**: The platform checks account balance or linked bank account
3. **Processing**: Transaction details are sent to payment processors via ACH network
4. **Settlement**: Funds are transferred between financial institutions
5. **Notification**: Both parties receive confirmation

This process relies heavily on the Automated Clearing House (ACH) network, which operates on business days only and can take 1-3 days for standard transfers. When users want instant transfers, platforms charge premium fees (typically 1-3%) to use debit card networks instead.

### Blockchain-Based P2P Innovation: How Send.it Eliminates Intermediaries

Send.it operates on a fundamentally different architecture using blockchain technology:

**Decentralized Processing**: Instead of routing through multiple banks and processors, transactions are verified by network nodes using smart contracts. This eliminates the need for traditional intermediaries and their associated delays.

**24/7 Operation**: Unlike traditional banking systems that close on weekends and holidays, blockchain networks operate continuously. Send.it users can send money instantly at 3 AM on Sunday with the same speed as Tuesday afternoon.

**Smart Contract Automation**: Automated contracts execute payments when predetermined conditions are met, reducing processing time from days to seconds while maintaining security through cryptographic verification.

For example, when Sarah sends $50 to her roommate through Send.it, the transaction is verified by the blockchain network in real-time, settled immediately, and recorded permanently—all without involving banks, ACH networks, or premium instant transfer fees.
